Internal Prep / Conversation Guide

Thomson Reuters – Architecture Briefing

Guide for bringing the Lead Architect and Director of Solution Architects up to speed before the Thomson Reuters architecture discussion.

Purpose of this conversation: Get the architecture team familiar with the customer ask, the context behind it, and the intended direction of the upcoming Thomson Reuters discussion.

1. Start With the Context

“I was asked to take the information captured in the Thomson Reuters Xperience action tracker, along with what I can recall from the customer conversation, and turn that into an Architect request. I did not originate the discovery, so I want to give you the context as accurately as I can before we discuss how we approach the customer.”

Customer sentiment

  • Very positive response to Genesys Xperience in Las Vegas.
  • Jake and Jeb specifically called out Dave Gussin as a favorite presenter.
  • They want to continue the conversation around the methodologies and architectural thinking presented at Xperience.

Why the conversation is broader

  • This is not intended to replace active implementation work.
  • The customer is asking for a broader architectural perspective.
  • The goal is to understand how today's decisions position them for future adoption.

2. What Thomson Reuters Is Actually Asking For

“Jake wants to understand how absolutely everything ties together in the overall big picture.”

The conversation should be centered on the relationship between major areas of the Genesys Cloud platform rather than treating each capability as a separate topic.

Architect Routing / Orchestration WEM Genesys Copilot AI / Intelligence Analytics Knowledge Data Configuration / Development

3. The Core Customer Concern

Positioning: This is not intended to be a feature-by-feature product presentation or a roadmap session. Thomson Reuters wants to understand the architectural strategy and methodology behind the platform so they can make the right foundational decisions now without unintentionally limiting what they can adopt later.

Their concern is essentially future readiness: they do not want to make decisions today that create limitations or unnecessary rework as their Genesys Cloud environment evolves.

4. The Foundation Example: Flow Milestones & Flow Outcomes

One point Thomson Reuters specifically acknowledged is that they need to establish the right foundations before they can fully expand into more advanced capabilities.

Use this as the example
Flow milestones and flow outcomes are an example of the underlying instrumentation and foundation they need to think about now in order to support future intelligence, automation, orchestration, analytics, and AI use cases.

The broader question for the Architect is: What foundational architectural decisions and practices should Thomson Reuters establish now, and how do those decisions connect to the larger Genesys Cloud model?

5. Connect the Ask Back to the Existing Work

The Xperience action tracker already contains several related workstreams. The point is not to cover each one individually, but to show that there is an emerging need for a unifying architecture conversation.

  • Navigator / Kaleidoscope and routing
  • Building from Spec
  • Intent Intelligence
  • CX as Code
  • Rules Based Decisions
  • WFM adoption
  • AI / QM initiatives
  • Existing AVA / AI-related work

The customer is increasingly interested in how these types of efforts fit together rather than becoming disconnected individual initiatives.

6. What I’d Like From the Architecture Team

Customer conversation

  • What should we explain about the overall architectural model?
  • Which concepts are most important to establish first?
  • How should we frame dependencies and sequencing?
  • How do we keep this strategic rather than turning it into a product tour?

Internal alignment

  • Who is the right resource to lead this?
  • Should Dave Gussin participate?
  • What existing Genesys work should we bring into the discussion?
  • What should the customer leave understanding?

7. Important Guardrail

Keep the conversation architecture-focused. Thomson Reuters is asking for methodology, relationships between capabilities, foundational decisions, and sequencing—not a catalog of features or a promise about future roadmap items.

8. Proposed Customer Outcome

By the end of the session: Thomson Reuters should have a clearer understanding of how “everything ties together,” what foundational decisions matter now, and how their current Genesys Cloud work fits into a broader architectural direction.

9. Close the Internal Briefing With These Questions

Question 1
What is the simplest architectural story we can tell that will give Thomson Reuters the “big picture” they are looking for?
Question 2
What foundational areas should they prioritize now to avoid unnecessary rework later?
Question 3
What should Dave / the Architect own in the conversation versus the existing account and project teams?
Desired internal outcome: Leave aligned on the customer storyline, the Architect’s role, and the few architectural concepts we need to make sure Thomson Reuters understands during the upcoming session.
