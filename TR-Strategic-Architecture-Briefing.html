Section 3

The Core Customer Concern
Positioning

This is not intended to be a feature-by-feature product presentation or a roadmap session. Thomson Reuters wants to understand the architectural strategy and methodology behind the platform so they can make the right foundational decisions now without unintentionally limiting what they can adopt later.

Their concern is essentially future readiness: they do not want to make decisions today that create limitations or unnecessary rework as their Genesys Cloud environment evolves.

Section 4

The Foundation Example: Flow Milestones & Flow Outcomes

One point Thomson Reuters specifically acknowledged: the right foundations need to be in place before they can fully expand into more advanced capabilities.

Use this as the example

Flow milestones and flow outcomes are an example of the underlying instrumentation and foundation they need to think about now in order to support future intelligence, automation, orchestration, analytics, and AI use cases.

The broader question for the Architect is: what foundational architectural decisions and practices should Thomson Reuters establish now, and how do those decisions connect to the larger Genesys Cloud model?

Section 7

SC Recommendations — Storyline, Concepts, Connections

My recommended positions on the three open alignment items from Section 6 — offered as the assigned SC, not as customer-stated requirements.

SC recommendation my professional view, not yet agreed internally Open item still needs internal confirmation

Core storyline SC recommendation

A five-beat arc: momentum → reframe → proof point → breadth → ask.

  1. Open with Xperience momentum TR's positive experience and specific call-out of Dave Gussin is the reason this conversation exists — use it to show the customer, not Genesys, is driving the ask.
  2. Reframe immediately State plainly this is not a feature tour or roadmap session. Doing this early keeps the Architect from defaulting to a product-demo posture.
  3. Anchor on one concrete example Flow milestones / flow outcomes as the proof point TR already recognizes. This is the one place we have a direct customer acknowledgment to lean on, so it should carry the most weight in the room.
  4. Widen the lens TR's own active workstreams already span the platform — the architecture conversation connects existing scope, it doesn't introduce new scope.
  5. Close on the ask Frame the Architect's job as helping TR sequence correctly now, not sell them something new.
Recommendation

Keep this order for the internal briefing and carry it into the eventual TR session — internal alignment should mirror what we intend to say externally.

Foundational concepts SC recommendation

Rather than treating all nine identified platform areas as equally weighted, structure them in three tiers so the Architect knows what to lead with.

Tier 1 — the flagship example

Concept
Flow milestones & flow outcomes (instrumentation)
Why lead here
The one foundational concept TR has already self-identified as a gap. Leading here shows we listened rather than pitched.

Tier 2 — the connective tissue (where "big picture" gets answered)

Concepts
Routing / orchestration as the backbone other domains plug into; reusable orchestration patterns and governance; knowledge strategy — increasingly the dependency for AI / Copilot work, given the AVA + Knowledge Fabric workstream
Why here
TR asked to understand relationships between domains, not each domain individually — this tier is where that question actually gets answered.

Tier 3 — supporting domains, reference only if asked

Concepts
WEM, Analytics, Data, Configuration / Development, Genesys Copilot, AI / Intelligence as individual areas
